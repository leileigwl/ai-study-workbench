# Track C Adapter: Books, Humanities & Business Frameworks

> **Ideal for**: Non-fiction books, economics, product strategy, leadership, law/policy, psychology, history, or any conceptual domain without automated unit tests.

---

## Design Philosophy: Escape Highlighting Graveyards via Transfer & Friction

Highlighting a book creates zero durable skill: readers nod along with smooth prose and forget the thesis two weeks later. Track C turns books into **actionable decision frameworks** by contrasting the author's model against common human intuition traps and testing whether the reader can apply it to an unfamiliar scenario.

---

## 5-Part Customization for Books & Frameworks

1. **Part 1 — Thesis Spine & Mental Model Map**:
   - Extract the book's core causal loop or 3–5 mental models (not chapter-by-chapter summaries).
   - Map how each model challenges conventional wisdom.
2. **Part 2 — Argument Reconstruction & Boundary Q&A**:
   - Ask *why* mechanisms work, *when they break* (boundary conditions where a famous framework fails), and *how competing theories disagree*.
   - Keep point-count rubrics separated in `answer_key.md`.
3. **Part 3 — Cognitive / Intuition Traps Table**:
   - `Domain | Naive Intuition | How It Backfires in Real Decisions | Disciplined Mental Model`.
4. **Part 4 — Cross-Context Case Transfer + Feynman Teach-Back**:
   - Present an unfamiliar scenario outside the book's own examples (e.g., applying Donella Meadows' leverage points to a failing engineering on-call rotation or a consumer subscription churn problem).
   - Require two outputs in the learner's own words:
     1. **Diagnose**: Identify which structural force or feedback loop drives the symptom.
     2. **60-Second Feynman Explanation**: Explain the core model to a smart peer with zero jargon.

---

## Grading Protocol

1. Score Part 2 drafts (`Matched: X / Y points`) without spoiling missed nuances; probe missed boundaries with a counterexample ("Under what condition would this rule make the situation worse?").
2. Grade Part 4 Case Transfer on three criteria in `answer_key.md`:
   - **Mechanism Accuracy** (correctly names the causal lever, not just surface symptoms),
   - **Boundary Awareness** (names unintended second-order effects),
   - **Jargon-Free Clarity** (Feynman criterion: plain language, concrete example).
