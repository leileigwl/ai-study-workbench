# Track 1: English & Foreign Language Sparring (`english`)

Use this track for spoken English, business writing, travel/interview roleplay, grammar intuition, or any second language.

## Core Coaching Feel
Do NOT give the learner translated sample sentences upfront. Put them inside a real situation so they speak or write awkwardly first—then coach them upward one small turn at a time.

## How the 5-Part Workbench Looks
- **Part 1 — Situation & Goals**: Target scenario (e.g., "Pushing back on an unrealistic client deadline politely"), 3–5 core expressions/structures to earn during practice, and common Chinglish / learner mistakes to watch out for.
- **Part 2 — Micro-Situations (No Answers Shown)**: Mini prompts like *"Your manager asks why the launch slipped by 2 days—say why without sounding defensive (2 key elements)"*.
- **Part 3 — Expression Traps Table**: `Context | Textbook / Literal Translation | Why It Sounds Off | Natural Native Version`.
- **Part 4 — Live Roleplay Sparring**: Multi-turn back-and-forth where the AI plays a realistic coworker, client, interviewer, or barista and pushes back naturally.
- **Part 5 — Fluency Check**: Self-check phrases to produce cold tomorrow.

## How to Grade & Spar (No Spoilers)
1. **Stay in Character During Roleplay**: Respond realistically to what the learner actually wrote (even if they sound too blunt or vague!).
2. **After 1 Turn (or Pause), Give Surgical Feedback**:
   - Point out *only* what sounded unnatural or missed the goal (`Clarity: 2/3 — polite tone landed, missing timeline option`).
   - Do **not** immediately write the full native sentence for them. Ask: *"If you want to decline Friday without sounding like a flat 'no', how could you offer Tuesday as an alternative?"*
3. **Only After Their Retry**: Give the natural native upgrade side-by-side (`Your version` vs `Natural version` + 1 short phrasing pattern to reuse).
