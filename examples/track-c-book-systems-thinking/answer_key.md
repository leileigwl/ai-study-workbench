### Q1.1 Why do managers often cause wilder boom-and-bust swings when they shorten reaction times or push harder on KPI quotas without fixing information delays?
- **Core Scoring Points (`3`)**:
  1. **Phase Lag / Overcorrection**: When feedback arrives late, decision-makers do not yet see the effect of prior actions, so they keep applying corrective force past the equilibrium point.
  2. **Reinforcing Amplification**: Aggressive KPI tuning inside a delayed loop amplifies oscillations rather than dampening them.
  3. **Remedy**: Either shorten the **physical/measurement delay**, widen **stabilizing buffers**, or dampen response aggressiveness relative to lag time.
- **Scenario Probes (Use if missed)**:
  - *Missing Point 1*: `"Think of adjusting a hotel shower with a 10-second pipe delay. If you crank the handle faster every second it still feels cold, what happens at second 11?"`

### Q1.2 In Meadows' Leverage Points hierarchy, why is changing the "rules or goal" of a system vastly more powerful than tuning parameters (e.g., bonus percentages or quota thresholds)?
- **Core Scoring Points (`2`)**:
  1. **Local vs Structural Scope**: Parameter changes scale quantitatively inside the existing feedback topology without altering actor incentives or constraints.
  2. **Goal/Rule Rewiring**: Changing rules or goals redirects *every* balancing and reinforcing loop simultaneously toward a different attractor.
- **Scenario Probes (Use if missed)**:
  - *Missing Point 2*: `"Why did increasing fines slightly for late daycare pickups fail compared to changing the pickup rule/norm itself?"`

### Transfer 4.1 Grading Rubric (Case Transfer + Feynman)
1. **Mechanism Accuracy**: Identifies **Rule Beating / Policy Resistance** (agents optimize "ticket closed count" by closing tickets prematurely or splitting replies, creating repeat tickets = reinforcing loop) plus **Shifting the Burden** (engineers fix symptoms instead of root bugs).
2. **Higher-Leverage Intervention**: Measures *first-contact resolution / root-cause bug reduction* and gives support direct escalation channels to product quality; watches out for second-order risk (e.g., slow responses if resolution-only metric encourages cherry-picking easy tickets).
3. **Feynman Check**: Explains delayed balancing loops using an everyday analogy (thermostat/shower/driving) with zero systems-engineering buzzwords.
