### Q1.1 Why are rent-controlled apartments much harder to find two years later?
- **Standard Answer Key Points (`3`)**:
  1. **Quantity Demanded Rises**: Cheaper rent pulls extra renters into the market (fewer roommates, staying longer).
  2. **Quantity Supplied Shrinks**: Builders halt new rentals; owners convert units to offices/condos or skip upkeep.
  3. **Non-Price Rationing Takes Over**: Shortages get rationed via long waitlists, personal connections, key-money bribes, or discrimination.
- **Follow-Up Probes if Missed (Never spoil directly)**:
  - *If missing Point 2*: `"Renters want cheaper apartments, sure—put yourself in a developer's shoes deciding whether to build 200 new apartments or luxury offices. What happens to new rentals in 2 years?"`
  - *If missing Point 3*: `"Say 1,000 renters line up for 100 cheap units. When price can't decide who gets the keys, how do landlords pick?"`

### Q1.2 Concert ticket prices triple overnight after an award win—did Supply shift or Demand shift?
- **Standard Answer Key Points (`2`)**:
  1. **Demand Curve Shifted Right**: Willingness to pay increased at every price due to external buzz.
  2. **Supply Quantity Stayed Fixed (Venue Seats)**: Higher price reflects movement along fixed stadium capacity, not supply shrinking.
- **Follow-Up Probes if Missed**:
  - *If confusing Supply vs. Demand*: `"Did the stadium lose seats overnight, or did twice as many buyers show up willing to pay?"`
