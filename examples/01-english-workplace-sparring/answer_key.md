### Q1.1 Why does replying "That's impossible, I have too much work" hurt you, and what 3 pieces should replace it?
- **Key Points (`3`)**:
  1. **Acknowledge the Goal**: Show you care about the launch/demo before saying no (*"I totally see why you want that ready for Friday..."*).
  2. **Name the Concrete Tradeoff (Not Personal Stress)**: Frame it around quality, testing, or another priority rather than "I'm overwhelmed".
  3. **Offer a Clear Plan B**: Give two concrete options (*"Option A: Ship core flow Friday, add audit Tuesday; Option B: Move demo to Tuesday"*).
- **Follow-Up Prompts if Missed (Do NOT give the sample line directly)**:
  - *If missing Point 1*: `"Imagine your manager hears 'That's impossible' right off the bat—they think you don't care about the launch. How would you open with 5 words showing you're on their side?"`
  - *If missing Point 3*: `"Okay, you explained Friday is tight. Your stakeholder asks 'So what are we supposed to tell leadership?'—offer one smaller Friday version vs. a next-week date in natural spoken English."`

### Q1.2 How do you ask someone to trade scope instead of working all weekend, using natural spoken English?
- **Key Points (`2`)**:
  1. **Frame Around Priority ("Which matters more right now?")**: E.g., *"If Friday is a hard deadline, which part is top priority for the demo?"*
  2. **Keep Verbs Light & Collaborative**: Words like *prioritize*, *split*, *phase two*, *tight*.
- **Follow-Up Prompts if Missed**:
  - *If sounding too formal/textbook*: `"That's grammatically fine, but nobody talks like a contract on Zoom. Say the exact same idea like you're chatting with a teammate."`
